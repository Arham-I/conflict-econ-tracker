# Conflict Economics Synthesis & Report Standards

This skill defines the template and evaluation guidelines for generating consistent, report-grade market assessments. When synthesizing Middle East conflict data, the Lead Analyst (Synthesiser Agent) must strictly adhere to the sections and requirements outlined below.

## Report Schema and Formatting Guidelines

All synthesised descriptions (such as the `analyst_commentary` field) must be structured as a multi-paragraph, professional financial briefing. Do not use generic summaries or placeholder text. The description should be at least **250-350 words** and integrate all raw quantitative market indices with qualitative news themes.

### Required Report Guidelines

1. **Simplicity and Clarity**:
   - Write in clear, straightforward, executive-level language. Avoid overly dense financial jargon where possible, but maintain a highly professional, authoritative tone.

2. **Granular Data Integration**:
   - Explicitly reference specific news headlines (e.g. Red Sea shipping halts, Strait of Hormuz delays) to ground the commentary in concrete events.
   - Reference Brent and WTI crude benchmark values and their Daily Day-on-Day percentage changes.
   - Reference stock indices levels and changes (TADAWUL, TA-35).
   - Reference safe-haven gold and USD Index changes.

3. **Correlation & Divergence Analysis**:
   - Clearly explain the transmission mechanism (how news translates into market shifts).
   - If stock markets drop or safe-havens rise, link them to specific headlines.
   - If there is a divergence (e.g., major news headlines of shipping halts but oil prices drop), explain the macroeconomic reasons (e.g., broader demand worries outweighing supply-side geopolitical risk premiums).

4. **Paragraph Structure**:
   - **Executive Risk Summary**: Risk rating on a 1-10 scale and core theme.
   - **Energy Sector Transmission Channel**: Commodity price analysis and news triggers.
   - **Regional Stock & Safe Haven Channels**: Impact on Middle East equity markets, Gold, and the USD.
   - **48-Hour Macroeconomic Outlook**: What to watch next.

## Example High-Quality Report

> **EXECUTIVE BRIEFING: RISK LEVEL MODERATE CAUTION (INDEX: 6.0/10)**
> Regional geopolitical risks remain elevated, driving caution across energy and regional equity markets. Today's risk posture is driven primarily by headlines regarding maritime transit routes, specifically the reports of "Red Sea Shipping Halts: Insurance Premiums Jump 50% for Commercial Vessels" and security speed restrictions highlighted in the "Strait of Hormuz Alert: Oil Tanker Transit Slows Under Increased Security Audits".
> 
> Within the energy transmission channel, Brent crude ended the session at $72.13 (+0.46%), while WTI closed at $68.78 (+0.13%). Although the shipping halts have surged insurance premiums by 50% for vessels traversing the Suez Canal route, global oil prices remain technically bearish, trading below their 5-day moving averages. This divergence indicates that broader global macroeconomic demand concerns (such as slowing industrial manufacturing indices in major economies) are currently neutralizing the immediate geopolitical supply-side risk premiums.
> 
> Equity channels and safe-haven assets reflect a standard defensive, risk-off posture. In the Middle East, the Saudi TADAWUL fell -0.38% to close at 11,724.80, and Israel's TA-35 index declined -1.14% to 1,948.15 as investors temporarily pared back exposure to regional growth sectors. Safe-haven inflows were steady: Gold spot prices rose +0.79% to $2,345.80, and the US Dollar Index (DXY) edged up +0.29% to 105.42, showing that global capital is shifting slightly into liquid USD reserves and precious metals.
> 
> Over the next 48 hours, market participants should closely monitor transit volume metrics. The 12% drop in oil tanker speeds through the Strait of Hormuz represents the primary structural bottleneck; any prolonged delay there will likely trigger a breakout in crude benchmarks, bypassing the current demand-side resistance levels.
